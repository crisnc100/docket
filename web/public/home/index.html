Property documents filed to the right unit, or held for a person.

Docket reads incoming leases, invoices and notices, and matches each one to a known unit. It files a document on its own only when two independent clues agree. A language model can suggest a unit to a reviewer; it never decides.

How a document is decided

Docket looks for clues in the text that point at one of the known units. Each kind of clue carries a weight. A close spelling counts for part of its weight.

A document is filed on its own only when at least two different clues point at the same unit, their weights add up to at least 0.586, and nothing points at a second unit. One clue alone, even the unit number, is never enough, so a document cannot file itself just by naming a unit.

Everything else waits on the review page, where a person sees the PDF, the clues, the rules' top pick and the model's suggestion side by side, and picks the unit or dismisses the document.

The model labels the document type and may suggest a unit. Its suggestion is shown to the reviewer and recorded in the trace; it is never used to file.

The review page

Held documents wait in one queue. Each shows the PDF, why it is waiting, every candidate unit with its score, the rules' top pick and the model's suggestion. A reviewer approves either, picks another candidate, or dismisses the document with a reason. Every step is in the document's trace.

It needs a reviewer token. To try it, run Docket locally with the quickstart, then open the review page.

Docket is open source under Apache-2.0. Every unit, tenant and document here is synthetic.
